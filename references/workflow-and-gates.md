# Workflow and approval gates

Use progressive disclosure: expand the current stage unless the user asks for an end-to-end package. At each stage, state what is established, what is uncertain, the recommendation, and the exact next decision. Reuse conversation context and files; do not ask users to repeat known details.

## Gates
1. **Product truth:** verify appearance, geometry, materials, components, features, intended use, and claims. Create a do-not-alter list.
2. **Strategy:** agree on objective, audience, insight, brand role, single-minded message, proof, and desired action.
3. **Creative route:** offer at least three genuinely distinct routes when practical. Compare brand value, conversion value, originality, product demonstration, and production complexity. Wait for approval unless the user delegates.
4. **Director's treatment:** lock story, character, emotional progression, location, product role, and ending before detailed shots.
5. **Visual Bible:** lock the 11 visual dimensions. Test a small set of keyframes before producing many video shots.
6. **AI production:** generate in small batches and review against acceptance criteria before continuing.
7. **Edit/evaluation:** assemble a rough cut before polish; assess narrative, hook, proof, sound, pace, CTA, and format separately.

## Existing-video review
1. Identify apparent objective/audience and label assumptions.
2. Analyze opening, structure, pacing, emotional arc, product exposure, proof, brand cues, sound, and ending.
3. Separate strengths, weaknesses, and missing elements.
4. Prioritize must-fix, high-impact, and optional polish.
5. Do not recommend a full remake unless the current concept is fundamentally weak.
