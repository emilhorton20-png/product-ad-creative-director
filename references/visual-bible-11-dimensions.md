# Visual Bible — 11 cinematography dimensions

Define every dimension explicitly; a film-stock name is not a visual description.

1. **Imaging medium:** digital cinema, 35mm emulation, 16mm texture, medium-format stills, etc. Describe grain and tonal behavior; do not claim generated footage is literally film-shot.
2. **Exposure:** overall exposure, highlight retention, shadow detail, and texture in windows, lamps, reflective metal, pale walls, and product edges.
3. **White balance:** warm/cool bias and effects on daylight, practical lamps, wood, whites, and skin. Do not turn all whites orange.
4. **Contrast:** black floor, midtone contrast, highlight roll-off, shadow openness; preserve dark wood, black metal, and mesh detail.
5. **Saturation:** overall and selective saturation; keep wood believable and skin natural.
6. **Palette/materials:** dominant/supporting/accent colors and rendering of wood grain, coated metal, mesh, fabric, glass, and skin. Product color must remain faithful.
7. **Lighting:** motivated source, direction, size/softness, intensity, falloff, fill, rim, practicals, time of day.
8. **Lens/optics:** focal-length range, camera distance, perspective, distortion, close-focus/macro behavior.
9. **Depth of field:** focus plane, background separation, bokeh, and readability of selling features.
10. **Texture/image response:** grain size/intensity, sharpness, microcontrast, halation, motion blur, dynamic range. Avoid fake dirt and excessive grain.
11. **Composition/movement:** camera height, subject placement, negative space, leading lines, headroom, product visibility, and motivated camera movement.

## Home-product baseline (adapt, do not apply blindly)
- Natural, motivated light and credible domestic spaces.
- Tactile but accurate material detail.
- Balanced warm/cool tones and natural skin.
- Product geometry and useful details remain legible.
- Restrained, purposeful camera movement.
- Avoid shallow focus that hides product function.

## Prompt assembly order
Subject/product → exact action/composition → camera/lens/movement → lighting/environment → palette/tonal response → film/texture → continuity constraints → concrete exclusions.

## Reference discipline
For each reference image, state whether it controls composition, lighting, color, styling, or product geometry. Do not let a style reference override product identity.
